# Getting evidence into practice

How does research become something people can use where they work?

---

## What solving it would make possible

Evidence arrives with a plan for how it will be used, shaped by the barriers of the place it is going. Where that happened, results reached patients: in Australian stroke units, a package targeted at local barriers reduced death or dependency at 90 days by 15.7 percentage points.

---

## The three questions

Scale
- In 2021, about 4.5 billion people, more than half the world's population, were not fully covered by essential health services.
- Even in high-income systems, recommended or appropriate care was delivered in a little over half of measured cases: 54.9% across 30 conditions in the United States, and 57% of 35,573 encounters in Australia.

What could be: narrowing the gap reaches everyone the evidence was produced for.

Neglectedness
- Evidence on changing managers' and policy-makers' decisions is much weaker than for clinicians.
- Few studies of tailored approaches come from low-income countries.

What could be: the least-studied settings are where careful design adds most.

Solvability
- Audit and feedback improved recommended practice by a median 4.3 percentage points, educational meetings by 4.0 and outreach visits by 5.6.
- Educational meetings changed patient outcomes by a median of 0.1 points, and adding components did not reliably add effect.

What could be: tailoring to local barriers gave a slight improvement. The open task is learning which tailoring works, and why.

---

## Reading the figures

| Figure | Measures | Does not show |
|---|---|---|
| 4.5 billion not fully covered | A global estimate from WHO and World Bank monitoring of essential health service coverage | Whether the care people did receive was of good quality |
| 54.9% of recommended care | Care received against recommendations, across 30 conditions in the United States | Care in other countries, or care today |
| 57% appropriate | 35,573 encounters reviewed in Australia | A national census of care |
| "17 years from research to practice" | Several studies that reached a similar figure while measuring different stages | A single measured average |
| "85% of research is wasted" | A worked estimate of waste in how studies are designed, published and reported | Waste in uptake |
| "60-30-10" | A commentary drawing together studies of guideline care, low-value care and harm | A pooled estimate with confidence intervals |

---

## Theory of change

1. Inputs: up-to-date systematic reviews and local performance data. Assumes reviews, not single studies, are what to transfer.
2. Activities: diagnose local barriers with staff, then tailor teaching and support. Assumes tailoring to barriers adds effect.
3. Outputs: protocols, outreach, and feedback with targets and action plans. Assumes feedback works best with targets and a low baseline.
4. Outcomes: practitioners follow recommended practice more often, measured locally. Assumes typical gains of 4 to 6 percentage points.
5. Impact: better outcomes for patients and learners, not only changed behaviour. Assumes targeted change can reach outcomes, as it did in stroke care.

The weakest link is 4 to 5: educational meetings improved practice by a median 4.0 percentage points but patient outcomes by 0.1.

---

## Evidence from the field

QASC, Australian stroke units. In a cluster trial across 19 units and 1,696 patients, nurses led protocols for fever, blood sugar and swallowing, supported by barrier workshops, outreach, reminders and local champions. Death or dependency at 90 days fell by 15.7 percentage points. Mortality did not change, and patients with severe stroke were under-represented.

The "Literacy Octopus", England. 13,323 primary schools received research materials by post or with light-touch support. There was no effect on pupil attainment or on teachers' use of research, and about 60% of schools engaged little or not at all.

Research Learning Communities, England. Evidence champions in 119 schools increased teachers' engagement with research. Reading did not improve.

## Who leads this work

Cochrane's Effective Practice and Organisation of Care group, the Canadian Institutes of Health Research, the US National Institutes of Health's implementation science program, the Education Endowment Foundation, and implementation researchers. Design joins where evidence is turned into something a practitioner can use.

---

## What design cannot do alone

- The evidence itself. Research that was never usable cannot be rescued by its packaging. Held by researchers, funders and journals.
- Time and incentives. Schools in the Literacy Octopus trial cited limited time. Held by leaders and managers.
- Synthesis. Single studies tend to overstate effects, so the systematic review is the unit worth transferring. Held by review groups.

---

## Where effort counts for less, and for more

| Counts for less | Counts for more |
|---|---|
| Typical effects are small: 4 to 6 percentage points. | Small, reliable gains across a whole service add up. What would make them routine? |
| Some waste happens upstream, before uptake. | Which design decisions sit closest to the practitioner, where uptake is decided? |
| Sending evidence changed nothing. | What does active, barrier-targeted support look like at the scale of a sector? |

---

## Open questions

- Which kinds of tailoring work, and why?
- How far does evidence from health care apply to education and other sectors?
- How is change sustained once support ends? Little research addresses it.
