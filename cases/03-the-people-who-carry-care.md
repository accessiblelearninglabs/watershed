Watershed · Case 03 · Who carries it

# Preparing the people who carry care

How are community health workers and other non-specialist providers prepared, and supported after training?

---

## What solving it would make possible

Training and supervision designed as one programme, so skills hold in routine care. Where this was done, results were large: in a cluster trial in Zimbabwe, 13.7% of adults treated by trained, supervised community workers had depressive symptoms at six months, against 49.9% in enhanced usual care.¹

---

## The three questions

**Scale**
- WHO projects a needs-based shortfall of 11.1 million health workers by 2030, increasingly concentrated in the African and Eastern Mediterranean regions.²
- The estimate is modelled, and its method paper reports that it is highly sensitive to the assumptions used.³

*What could be:* well-prepared non-specialist providers extend care to places professionals cannot yet reach.

**Neglectedness**
- WHO's community health worker guideline found the evidence not granular enough to say which training and supervision approaches work best, and found no economic evaluations.⁴
- A review of 39 studies of the Project ECHO telementoring model found one that measured a change in provider behaviour, and six that measured patient outcomes.⁵

*What could be:* the open question is a training-design question, which is where design acts.

**Solvability**
- Across 82 trials, community-based workers (termed lay health workers in the review) improved childhood vaccination, breastfeeding and tuberculosis cure rates (moderate certainty). Effects on mortality were uncertain.⁶
- Training or supervision alone improved provider practice by about 10 to 16 percentage points; combined, about 18 to 19; group problem-solving, 28 to 38. Results varied widely.⁷

*What could be:* supervision designed with the training, not added after it.

## Theory of change

1. **Inputs:** funded trainers, a defined protocol, and supervisor time. *Assumes training and supervision are resourced together.*
2. **Activities:** brief, task-focused training with regular group supervision. *Assumes training plus supervision outperforms either alone.*⁷
3. **Outputs:** community health workers delivering a defined protocol, supported after training. *Assumes competency-based preparation is feasible.*⁴
4. **Outcomes:** provider practice improves and people's symptoms fall. *Assumes trial gains carry into routine care.*⁶
5. **Impact:** care reaches people where professionals are scarcest. *Assumes funded roles and supplies exist for trained workers.*

The weakest link is 4 to 5: it depends on funded posts, supplies and management support, which sit outside training.⁶ **[verify: add the WHO source on funded posts]**

## Evidence from the field

**Friendship Bench, Zimbabwe.** In a cluster trial across 24 Harare clinics, 573 adults received six sessions of problem-solving therapy from trained, supervised community workers. At six months, 13.7% had depressive symptoms against 49.9% in enhanced usual care. Follow-up was six months, and 86% of participants were women.¹

**Thinking Healthy Programme, rural Pakistan.** Lady Health Workers received two days of training, a one-day refresher and monthly half-day group supervision. Among 903 mothers, depression at six months fell to 23% against 53%.⁸ A seven-year follow-up found no lasting effects on the children.⁹

**Project ECHO.** In a review of 39 studies of this widely adopted telementoring model, most measured participation or knowledge.⁵

---

## Who leads this work

Community health workers themselves, national ministries and community health worker programmes, WHO's Health Workforce department and WHO Academy, the Centre for Global Mental Health, and the ECHO Institute. Design joins where preparation and supervision are planned.

---

## What design cannot do alone

- **Jobs.** Trained workers need funded posts. Held by ministries and funders. **[verify: add source]**
- **Supplies and system links.** Programmes depend on supplies and management support.⁶ Held by health systems.
- **Migration and retirement.** Held by workforce policy.

---

## Where effort counts for less, and for more

| Counts for less | Counts for more |
|---|---|
| Funded posts and supplies limit what trained workers can do. | Which partners hold posts and supplies, and how can training be planned alongside them? |
| E-learning and classroom teaching produce about the same knowledge gains.¹⁰ | Format can be chosen for reach and cost. What design choices change practice, not only knowledge? |
| The larger effects came from supervision and group problem-solving, beyond the training event.⁷ | Supervision is a design object. What would it take to design it as carefully as the training? |

---

## Open questions

- Do trial effects hold in routine services, which often provide less support?⁶
- Do short-term gains last? In Pakistan, effects did not reach the children seven years later.⁹
- Which approaches are cost-effective? Almost no evidence exists.⁴
